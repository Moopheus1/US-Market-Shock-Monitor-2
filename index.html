▸ signal framework — how the pre-market bias score works ▾
0–15 strong bearFutures sharply down + VIX up overnight.
16–39 bearishFutures net down overnight.
40–60 neutralNo strong overnight move.
61–69 bullishFutures net up overnight.
70–100 strong bullFutures sharply up + VIX down overnight.
What it is: a weighted average of the overnight % change in ES/NQ/YM/RTY futures, VIX (inverted), 10Y yield, dollar and crude, each measured against the prior 4pm ET close. Raw % changes are clamped to ±3 and mapped to 0–100 (50 = flat). Note: inputs are NOT individually scaled — VIX's % moves are several times larger than ES's, so VIX carries more influence than its weight suggests. What the honest backtest says (rebuilt hour-by-hour exactly as it read at 8pm SGT, ~600 trading days — see the Edge Tracker above): it is a good summary of the overnight move, so it tells you whether today will probably close red or green. It is not a forecast: from 8pm SGT to the close it is right about half the time, because the move it reads has already happened. The older "0.555 correlation / 78–96% hit rate" figures compared the score with the gap it was partly built from and are withdrawn.

▸ signal framework — how the signpost count works (backtested) ▾
<20% triggered — accumulateFew indicators flashing. Overweight equities, buy dips aggressively.
20–39% — maintainA handful of signposts firing, not yet broad-based. Hold positions, add hedges (puts/VIX calls).
40–59% — reduce riskClose to half of all signposts triggered. Raise cash to 20–30%, avoid new equity positions.
60%+ — deriskBroad-based, majority of signposts triggered. Sell aggressively, raise cash >50%, go defensive.
Each indicator triggers on LEVEL (in its own red zone), TREND (moved fast toward red recently, off real historical FRED values), or for VIX and treasury yields specifically, a third condition tuned for crash detection - see below. Either firing counts as triggered; the number shown is a straight count, not a blended average. Backtested against 2015-2026 (2,902 trading days, re-run July 2026 with publication-lag-aware, as-of-date data): the composite was re-validated against all 9 SPY drawdowns of 8%+ in the window. Slow, regime-driven events are detected well: 2015-16 growth scare peaked at 46%, COVID at 54%, and the 2022 bear market at 54%, against a calm-day median of 15% - readings of 40%+ occurred on only 0.9% of calm days, and 60%+ never occurred outside a genuine event. This re-run also caught and fixed three scoring bugs the previous version shipped with: CPI was scored on its raw index level against YoY-percentage zones (permanently triggered), the dollar index was scored on the broad DTWEXBGS scale against zones calibrated for ICE DXY (triggered on 94% of all days), and the treasury flight-to-safety collapse check referenced series that were never actually scored (dead code - now live via the two treasury tiles). Membership changes from the re-run: VIX term structure and NFCI promoted into the count (see their cards), credit spreads demoted to context (source data truncated at FRED; NFCI covers the same conditions channel with 50+ years of history), jobless claims and put/call evaluated and rejected (claims LAG equity corrections - 0.4x lift; put/call has a 2019-2026 data hole). Known, honest limitation: fast vol-shock corrections - Feb 2018, Q4 2018, Aug 2024, Feb-Apr 2025 - peak at only ~23% under this model. That is not a calibration miss; it is structural. A count of mostly-macro indicators cannot turn red in two weeks. This composite is a REGIME gauge: it tells you when the environment is fragile (2015-16, 2020, 2022 all crossed 40%), not when a crash starts. The two fastest inputs (VIX stress zones, VIX term structure) are the only ones that move intra-correction - watch those cards directly, and Part II above, for anything faster than a regime shift. Prior published figures (72.7% COVID peak, 2,896 days) could not be reproduced under the production scoring logic and are superseded by this run. This is a 2–4 week strategic read (FRED data has a 1-day lag) — it answers "are we in a risky regime," not "what happens today." For that, see Part II below.

This is NOT a monotonic risk gauge like VIX-level or credit spreads elsewhere in this dashboard, where higher always means more danger. It's U-shaped: risk reads come from either EXTREME, not from the number simply going up. A reading of 83 is in the normal 20–100 band — elevated within that band, but not the euphoric extreme (>100) that would trigger a contrarian derisk read. Thresholds (20 / 100) are a judgment call based on NAAIM's own historical range (all-time low ≈ -3.6, all-time high ≈ 120.6, mean ≈ 65) — not backtested the way Part I's signposts were.
